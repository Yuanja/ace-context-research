# BRIGHT

## Citation

Hongjin Su et al. "BRIGHT: A Realistic and Challenging Benchmark for Reasoning-Intensive Retrieval." arXiv:2407.12883; ICLR 2025 version on OpenReview/proceedings; v4 submitted 2025-03-26.

## Core Claim

BRIGHT is a retrieval benchmark where relevance requires reasoning beyond keyword or ordinary semantic matching. The paper frames this as "level 3" retrieval: the query and relevant document may be connected through an underlying principle, algorithm, theorem, causal mechanism, or analytical chain that is not explicit in surface text.

## Dataset

The arXiv abstract reports 1,384 real-world queries spanning domains such as economics, psychology, mathematics, and coding. The official site describes 1,385 real-world queries from StackExchange, LeetCode, and math competitions. Treat the 1,384/1,385 mismatch as a version/source-count discrepancy.

BRIGHT includes 12 short-document datasets and long-document variants. Short-document leaderboard scores are average nDCG@10 across 12 datasets.

## Baseline Findings

The paper reports that SFR-Embedding-Mistral scores 59.0 on the MTEB retrieval subset but only 18.3 nDCG@10 on BRIGHT. It also reports that using explicit LLM reasoning steps as the retrieval query improves performance by up to 12.2 points.

## Relevance To ACE

BRIGHT does not evaluate ACE-style playbook learning directly. It is relevant because it tests whether retrieval systems can reason about what evidence is needed. ACE could plausibly be evaluated on BRIGHT by learning reusable query-reasoning strategies, while ACM/ARC could preserve search traces and exact evidence during long retrieval runs.
