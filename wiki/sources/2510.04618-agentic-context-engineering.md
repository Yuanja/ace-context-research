# Agentic Context Engineering

## Citation

Qizheng Zhang et al. "Agentic Context Engineering: Evolving Contexts for Self-Improving Language Models." arXiv:2510.04618. Comment says ICLR 2026; v3 submitted 2026-03-29.

## Core Claim

ACE treats context as an evolving playbook that accumulates, refines, and organizes strategies through Generator, Reflector, and Curator roles. The paper argues this avoids two failure modes in prior context adaptation: brevity bias and context collapse.

## Method

- **Generator** produces task trajectories and marks useful or misleading context bullets.
- **Reflector** extracts concrete insights from successes, errors, execution feedback, and labels when available.
- **Curator** writes small delta context items that are merged into the playbook using non-LLM logic.
- Context is itemized into structured bullets with metadata rather than rewritten as one monolithic prompt.
- The system uses grow-and-refine: contexts expand, then redundant or stale items are consolidated.

## Evaluation Notes

The paper reports results on AppWorld and domain-specific finance benchmarks, with additional medical and text-to-SQL experiments in appendices. On AppWorld with DeepSeek-V3.1-671B, Table 1 reports ReAct baseline average 42.4, offline ReAct+ACE average 59.4 with labels, and online ReAct+ACE average 59.5 without labels. The paper summarizes this as an average agent improvement of +10.6% over selected baselines and +8.6% on finance.

## Limitations

The authors state ACE relies on a reasonably strong Reflector. If the Reflector cannot extract meaningful insights, the context can become noisy or harmful. They also note not all tasks benefit from rich context; some benefit more from concise high-level instructions or a single reusable rule.
